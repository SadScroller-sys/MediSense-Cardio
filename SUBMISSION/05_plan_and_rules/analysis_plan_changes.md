## 2026-11-10 -- Traffic-light safeguard (rules fixed BEFORE any evaluation on real captures or own photos)
- Tool: `safeguard/ecg_traffic_light.py` gives GREEN / AMBER / RED for one digitised 12-lead signal (1 kHz, microvolts, as the digitiser saves it). Uses only the digitised signal, never the reference.
- Score: the 15 output features (Section D2) -> logistic regression (same pipeline as Section L) trained ONCE on the simulated new 100 (images with a reading, label = wrong HR > 5 bpm, cleaned measure; Sections J/K). No other data is used for training or for choosing thresholds.
- Lights (thresholds chosen on the cross-validated out-of-fold scores of the simulated training set only, whole recordings held out, 5-fold x10): AMBER threshold = the highest score such that at least 90% of wrong readings have a score at or above it. RED threshold = the lowest score above which at least 50% of readings are wrong (out-of-fold). If no score satisfies the RED rule, RED = the 95th percentile of out-of-fold scores. A photo whose digitised signal gives no usable heart rate (fewer than 3 beats in lead II, or no lead II) is RED by rule ("no usable heart rate").
- Information only (do not change the light): reasons shown to the user (beat spacing irregular, leads disagree, detectors disagree), each when the feature is beyond the 90th percentile of the right readings in the simulated training set.
- Evaluation, done once after the tool is frozen: (a) real captures from the competition (Arm C 40 + extension 100 = 140 recordings; images with an output; cleaned labels), (b) the 60 own-phone photos. Report per light: number of readings, wrong-heart-rate rate with Wilson 95% interval, share of all wrong readings that land in AMBER+RED, share of right readings flagged; including no-output photos as RED in a second table. Descriptive; real captures hold fewer than 100 wrong readings. Packaged tool reproduces the cached features on 20 signals (tolerance 1e-6) or the build is not accepted.
- **Rule addition made 2026-11-10, BEFORE any evaluation on real captures or own photos (found by a unit test):** a signal of pure noise got GREEN (score 1e-12), because the logistic score extrapolates far outside the training range. Added an out-of-range guard: if any feature (except amp_log, which depends on the amplitude scale) lies outside the [min, max] seen in the simulated training set, a GREEN light becomes AMBER with the reason "signal looks unlike the data the tool was trained on". The guard is fixed now, before the evaluation, and is evaluated as part of the tool.

## 2026-11-14 -- A better QT method: developed on the first 50, frozen, then tested ONCE on the new 100 (rules fixed BEFORE any code ran; EXPLORATORY)
- Why: Section S (2026-11-13) found that NeuroKit2's wavelet methods do not give a usable QT even on the ORIGINAL signal (median absolute difference from 12SL 36 ms; only 69% of recordings gave a value). The user asked to try a better method. PR and QRS are NOT part of this step.
- New method (my own code, `scripts/analysis/section_T_better_qt.py`), all constants fixed now: light smoothing of 10 ms; the beat anchor is the R-peak of lead II (nk.ecg_clean + nk.ecg_peaks on lead II); for each lead, the beats whose window [R - 300 ms, R + Tmax] lies inside that lead's finite window (Tmax = min(600 ms, 0.7 x median RR)) are cut out and the pointwise MEDIAN beat is built (one beat is enough). QRS onset = going backwards from the steepest upslope within 60 ms before the R-peak until the absolute slope falls below 5% of the largest slope in [R - 60 ms, R + 60 ms] (at most 150 ms before R). Baseline = median of [onset - 40 ms, onset - 10 ms]. T peak = largest absolute deviation from the baseline in [R + 100 ms, R + Tmax]. T end by TWO alternatives: (tangent) the intersection with the baseline of the tangent at the steepest slope on the descending side of the T wave within 200 ms after the peak; (threshold) the first point after the peak where the deviation falls to 15% of the peak. Per lead QT = T end - onset.
- Three aggregators over leads: (A) lead II only; (B) the median over leads of the per-lead QT; (C) global = (90th percentile of the T-end times) - (10th percentile of the QRS-onset times), both relative to R. Candidates = 3 aggregators x 2 T-end methods = 6. For photos each non-II lead covers 2.5 s; B and C need at least 4 leads with a value, else "no value".
- DEVELOPMENT (first 50 recordings, ORIGINAL 500 Hz signals only, the first 50 have no saved digitised signals): signals are cut to the page layout so that the test is like a photo (columns I-II-III 0 to 2.5 s, aVR-aVL-aVF 2.5 to 5, V1-V3 5 to 7.5, V4-V6 7.5 to 10; lead II the full 10 s). Reference = 12SL QT_Int_Global. Selection rule: among candidates that give a value for at least 90% of recordings, choose the one with the smallest median absolute difference after subtracting a single constant offset (the median difference on these 50 recordings); ties go to the higher Spearman rho. The chosen candidate AND its offset are written to `results/analysis/sectionT_frozen.json`. After that nothing is changed.
- TEST, run ONCE after freezing: the new 100 (21205 excluded as before). (a) the frozen method on the ORIGINAL 500 Hz signal cut to the same windows as the digitised leads; (b) the frozen method on the DIGITISED clean page (primary); same statistics as Section S. Secondary, descriptive: the other 12 versions by damage and the GREEN images (as in Section S).
- Acceptance rule (unchanged, still the assistant's proposal and not approved by the guide): median absolute difference <= 20 ms AND at least 90% within +-40 ms AND |bias| <= 10 ms; "validated" only if (a) AND (b) meet it AND at least 90% of recordings have a value. If it is not met, QT stays "Not measured" and the numbers are reported as they are.
- Expectation written before running: the original-signal QT with NeuroKit2 was 36 ms off; a median-beat tangent method is expected to improve on that but may still miss the 20 ms rule, because 12SL itself differs from Glasgow by 4 to 6.5 ms and because a photo adds noise. The bias offset is learned on 50 recordings only.
- Cautions to state: development on 50 recordings and 6 candidates allows mild overfitting; the new 100 were used in Section S with a different method, but nothing here was tuned on them; one digitiser; simulated pages; 12SL is an algorithm, not an expert measurement.

## 2026-11-14 (later still) -- PR interval and QRS duration with my own median-beat method: development on the first 50, frozen, tested ONCE on the new 100, confirmed ONCE on new phone photos (rules fixed BEFORE any code ran; EXPLORATORY)
- Why: the user asked to also make PR and QRS measurable. With NeuroKit2 both failed even on the original signal (Section S: PR median abs diff 78 ms, QRS 84 to 98 ms). Same discipline as QT (Section T): nothing is changed after a result is seen.
- **Physical limit, stated before the run:** at 200 dpi one pixel is about 5 ms (METHODS section 2). Onset and offset each carry about +-5 ms from the pixels alone, so a QRS duration cannot be much better than about +-7 ms even for a perfect reader; the QRS rule of 10 ms median error is close to this limit and blurred or small photos cannot meet it. - Code: `scripts/analysis/section_U_pr_qrs.py` reusing the beat cutting of Section T (the beat anchor, smoothing 10 ms, median beat from [R - 300 ms, R + Tmax], same windows, same constants). New constants, all fixed now: QRS offset = going forward from the steepest downslope within 60 ms after the R-peak until the absolute slope falls below a fraction of the largest slope, at most 150 ms after R; the fraction is 5% or 10% (two alternatives, used for both onset and offset). P wave = the largest absolute deviation from the baseline (median of [QRS onset - 40 ms, QRS onset - 10 ms]) in [QRS onset - 250 ms, QRS onset - 30 ms]; a P wave is accepted only if it is at least 0.02 mV high and its peak is more than 10 ms from the start of that window. P onset by (tangent) the intersection with the baseline of the tangent at the steepest upslope within 100 ms before the P peak, or (threshold) the first point going backwards from the peak where the deviation falls to 15% of the P peak. QRS = offset - onset; PR = QRS onset - P onset (per lead).
- Aggregators over leads, as in Section T: (A) lead II only; (B) the median over leads (at least 4 leads with a value); (C) global: QRS = 90th percentile of the offset times - 10th percentile of the onset times; PR = 10th percentile of the QRS onset times - 10th percentile of the P onset times. QRS candidates = 3 x 2 slope fractions = 6; PR candidates = 3 x 2 P-onset methods = 6.
- DEVELOPMENT: the first 50 recordings, ORIGINAL 500 Hz signals cut to the page layout (as in Section T). References = 12SL QRS_Dur_Global and PR_Int_Global (recordings without a 12SL PR value, about 9%, are excluded from the PR comparison and from its value share). Selection rule per parameter: among candidates with a value for at least 90% of the recordings that have a reference, choose the smallest median absolute difference after subtracting one constant offset (the median difference on these 50); ties go to the higher Spearman rho. Choice and offset are written to `results/analysis/sectionU_frozen.json`; then nothing changes.
- TEST ONCE: the new 100 (21205 excluded): (a) the frozen method on the ORIGINAL 500 Hz signal in the photo windows, (b) on the DIGITISED clean page (primary). CONFIRMATION ONCE: the 100 own-phone photos (50 recordings), straightened (V2) primary, original (V0) secondary, method and offsets unchanged.
